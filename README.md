A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one funny tombstone epitaph to
`/output/entry.txt` — the famous last words of someone killed by their own cooking.

## The line

> Here I lie, killed by my own cooking. The recipe said serves four. It served one.

81 characters, 81 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: a one-line tombstone epitaph of famous
last words; a real laugh on one read; a surprise, twist or double meaning;
reads like something carved in stone; stays in the head.

- **Epitaph first.** "Here I lie" is the classic epitaph opening — the dead
  speaking from the stone, flat and permanent, before the joke even starts.
- **A real laugh, not a pun for its own sake.** The recipe promised to feed
  four people. It fed one — to death. The joke is the body count hiding inside
  a serving suggestion.
- **A double meaning that lands cleanly.** "Serve" means to feed, and means to
  be a portion for. "It served one" means both at once: one portion, one victim.
  No explanation needed; it lands on the first read.
- **Carved, not explained.** One short line, no stage directions, no quotes, no
  setup beyond the stone itself. Nothing to trim.
- **Quotable.** "It served one" is the kind of punchline you repeat to a friend
  at a funeral — which is exactly where it belongs.

Chosen from 10 candidates head-to-head against the judge brief; the full
comparison is in
`u-riidzhsn-famous-last-words-the-funniest-epitaph-ff177406-candidates.md`.

